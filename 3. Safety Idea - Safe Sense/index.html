Multi-Sensor Based Automated Personal Safety System with Context-Aware Threat Detection and Adaptive Emergency Response.

An autonomous layer of personal protection

A safety system designed to recognize distress when manual action may not be possible.

This invention relates to a personal safety system integrated within a mobile device or wearable apparatus that automatically detects potential user distress through multi-sensor data analysis. Rather than depending exclusively on a manually activated emergency button, the system continuously observes relevant signals and identifies patterns associated with an unsafe or threatening event.

The invention combines motion sensing, user interaction patterns, and environmental audio analysis to compute a real-time threat assessment score. A processing unit evaluates these independent data streams in context, allowing the system to distinguish routine activity from circumstances that may require assistance.

“The system is configured to act as an intelligent companion—assessing changing circumstances, verifying urgency where possible, and initiating an appropriate emergency response without requiring the user to navigate a device.”

Field of the invention.

Intelligent, automated emergency detection and response for personal safety.

The present invention generally concerns personal safety, emergency communications, wearable computing, mobile-device intelligence, and context-aware sensor processing. More particularly, it concerns an intelligent, automated emergency detection and response system employing behavioral, environmental, and sensor-based data processing to identify a potential safety threat and coordinate a responsive action.


The system may be implemented in smartphones, smartwatches, fitness bands, personal locator devices, or other connected wearable apparatuses capable of collecting sensor information and transmitting communications to designated contacts, monitoring services, or emergency responders.

The gap between danger and a manual alert.

Existing solutions often place the responsibility for activation on the person who may be unable to act.

Manual activation dependence

Many personal safety applications and devices rely on a panic button, gesture, or on-screen command. In an incident involving surprise, injury, intimidation, or physical restraint, the user may be unable to deliberately trigger that mechanism.

Isolated signals lack context

A sudden movement, loud sound, or missed interaction alone may occur during normal life. Systems based on one signal can either miss genuine distress or generate disruptive, unnecessary alerts.

Delayed awareness and evidence loss

When an event is reported only after the fact, important location, timing, and environmental context may be unavailable to those attempting to assist the user.

Need for adaptive detection

An improved system is needed that can autonomously detect distress, corroborate conditions across multiple sources, reduce false alarms, and take action with minimal user intervention.

Five coordinated components. One informed decision.

The architecture fuses device-level sensing and communication capabilities into a context-aware safety workflow.

Motion sensing module

Captures acceleration, orientation, impact, stillness, gait disruption, and unusual movement sequences.

User interaction monitoring

Evaluates interaction cadence, failed unlocking, abnormal inactivity, repeated gestures, and direct user cancellation.

Environmental audio analysis

Identifies acoustic features associated with distress, elevated conflict, impact sounds, or a user-defined emergency keyword.

Processing unit

Fuses inputs, applies contextual logic and confidence weighting, and continuously calculates a threat assessment score.

Communication module

Securely transmits alerts, live location, selected evidence, and response updates via available network pathways.

From data acquisition to adaptive response.

A measured escalation model balances urgent action with user control and false-alarm prevention.

Continuous data acquisition

The system acquires motion, device interaction, audio, temporal, and optionally location-related information. Data can be processed locally where supported, with only relevant event information prepared for communication.

Threat detection and assessment

Detected features are compared against expected or learned usage patterns. A sudden impact followed by stillness, distressed vocal characteristics, abnormal interaction interruption, or multiple concurrent anomalies may increase the threat assessment score.

Triggering conditions

When the score exceeds a first threshold, the device may issue a discreet haptic, visual, or audible check-in. If the user does not respond, if corroborating signals persist, or if a higher critical threshold is reached, the system escalates.

Emergency response actions

The communication module can send alerts to trusted contacts or emergency services, share live location, open an emergency communication channel, and retain time-stamped contextual evidence according to configured permissions.

Safety designed for the real world.

Key benefits arise from the system's ability to observe, interpret, verify, and respond.

Automatic distress detection

Detects potential danger even when the user cannot access a manual control.

False alarm reduction

Uses multiple contextual signals instead of reacting to isolated events.

Real-time evidence collection

Preserves relevant, time-stamped context to support a fast, informed response.

Enhanced response speed

Automates communication and location sharing during a critical window.

Mobile & wearable adaptable

Fits naturally within smartphones and connected wearable technology.

When a person cannot call for help, their device can recognize the need.

By integrating multi-sensor analysis, context-aware threat detection, false-alarm reduction, and adaptive emergency response, this invention improves user safety and directly addresses the limitations of traditional manual activation systems.
